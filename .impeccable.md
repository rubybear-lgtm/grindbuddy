# Grind Buddy — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users
Engineers at any level (student through senior) who are actively practicing LeetCode problems for technical interviews. Their mental state: focused and motivated, but aware that grinding randomly doesn't work — they want to study smarter. They're time-constrained and impatient with fluff. They respond to tools that feel sharp and purposeful, not toys or generic SaaS.

### Brand Personality
**Sharp · Focused · Relentless**

The voice is terse, direct, and earned — like a training tool built by someone who's been through the process. It never wastes words. It respects the user's time and intelligence. No hype, no hand-holding. Copy should feel like a well-written terminal man page crossed with a focused study guide.

Emotional goal: the user should feel *in control* and *clear-headed* — not overwhelmed by 2000 problems, not anxious about what to practice next. Grind Buddy removes the decision fatigue and just tells you what to do today.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Keep the existing dark theme.** The visual style is already right:
- Dark backgrounds (near-black), high contrast
- Allium green-teal primary (`oklch(0.82–0.85 0.18 165)`) with brand cyan as secondary accent
- Bold, uppercase, tight-tracked headings (Outfit font)
- Inter for body copy, JetBrains Mono for code/data values
- Bento grid layouts, glassmorphism panels, subtle shimmer animations
- Minimal decorative elements — geometry over illustrations

**References**: The existing Grind Buddy aesthetic is the reference. Closest external analogues: Linear.app (dark, precise, purposeful) and Vercel's marketing sites (technical, developer-native).

**Anti-references**:
- Generic SaaS: gradient heroes, stock photos, "revolutionize your workflow" copy
- Enterprise dashboards: gray, flat, data-heavy, corporate-feeling
- Gamified study apps: bright colors, mascots, Duolingo energy
- DevOps infra tools: the copy can reference infra themes subtly but should never *read* like an infrastructure product (no `DEPLOY_AGENT`, `INDEX_PROBLEM`, `ENGINE_LATENCY`)

### Design Principles

1. **Copy names the thing.** UI text should say what the feature actually does — not what it metaphorically resembles. "Due for review" not "Priority Recall Stream." The aesthetic can be bold and technical; the words must be clear.

2. **Visual boldness, verbal restraint.** Heavy typography, high contrast, and motion are welcome. Copy should be terse — fewer words, more impact. Never pad a headline.

3. **Respect the user's intelligence.** No tooltips explaining what a streak is. No onboarding walkthroughs for obvious features. Users are engineers who know LeetCode — write for them.

4. **Dark mode is the canonical experience.** The app interior is dark-first. Light mode (landing page) should feel like a natural extension, not an afterthought. When in doubt, optimize for dark.

5. **Function first, then delight.** Motion and shimmer effects serve attention and feedback — not decoration. If an animation doesn't communicate state or guide attention, remove it.

- Primary: `oklch(0.85 0.18 165)` — Allium green
- Brand cyan: `oklch(0.75 0.15 190)`
- Background dark: `oklch(0.10 0 0)`
- Card dark: `oklch(0.14 0.01 165)`
- Heading font: Outfit (black, uppercase, tight)
- Body font: Inter
- Mono font: JetBrains Mono
